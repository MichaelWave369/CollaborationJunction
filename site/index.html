01 / DEFINING THE SUBJECT

First, define
the machine.

Not everything automated is artificial intelligence. And not every AI system generates images or prose.

Read the OECD's definition ↗
WORKING DEFINITION
Machine-based systems that infer from their inputs how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions.

Adapted from the OECD's 2024 explanatory memorandum. Different systems have different levels of autonomy and adaptiveness.

01

Rule-based software

Explicit instructions and deterministic operations. Automation alone is not proof of AI.

02

Machine learning

Models fit patterns in data for prediction, classification or related inference tasks.

03

Generative AI

Models produce text, imagery, audio, code or other content. One category, not all of AI.

04

AI agents

Systems coordinating objectives, models and tools. Agency as design is not proof of consciousness.

04 / RESEARCH WITHOUT THE HYPE

We measure.
We question.
We keep receipts.

Claim provenance, baselines, negative results and independent understanding belong in the same ledger as successful demos.

01

Separate claims from observations

A product page demonstrates a documented assertion. Reproducible testing demands more.

02

Test the counterargument

Include human-only baselines, null results, and cases where AI makes things worse.

03

Record human contributions

Document task selection, corrections, validation, judgment and post-task comprehension.

04

Respect real boundaries

No personal data harvesting, unauthorized reverse engineering or participant studies without consent.
