# Research Charter · CJ-000

**Version:** 0.1.0 (2026-10-12)  
**Status:** Foundational policy, not peer-reviewed research.

## Mission

Determine which AI capabilities are demonstrably present in contemporary technologies, and which measurable outcomes arise when people use AI in different kinds of work.

## Questions

1. What qualifies as an AI system, versus ordinary deterministic automation?
2. What exact product feature is supported by which public evidence?
3. Which human contributions can be observed in a human–AI workflow?
4. Under which conditions does AI assistance improve, worsen, or fail to change outcomes?
5. How do accuracy, novelty, skill acquisition, time, and independent understanding change?

## Working terms

- **AI system:** a machine-based system that infers from inputs how to generate outputs, such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions. This follows the OECD's updated 2024 explanation; see https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/03/explanatory-memorandum-on-the-updated-oecd-definition-of-an-ai-system_3c815e51/623da898-en.pdf
- **Rule-based automation:** executes explicit human-defined logic; not automatically AI simply because it is sophisticated.
- **Machine learning:** develops predictive or generative behavior through fitted models.
- **Generative AI:** produces content based on learned representations; it is a subset of AI, not a synonym for all AI.
- **AI agent:** an orchestration pattern connecting model(s), objectives, memory/state, and tools. Agentic behavior does not establish consciousness.
- **Human–AI collaboration:** a workflow with observable reciprocal iteration, including human selection, verification, correction, or direction.
- **AI-assisted output:** an outcome produced using AI in any capacity. Assistance alone does not establish authorship quality, originality, or reliability.

## Evidence tiers

- **DOCUMENTED:** primary-source description of a specific deployed feature. Verifies a public statement exists, not the vendor's inaccessible implementation.
- **OBSERVED:** an authorized direct test of behavior, with reproducible logs.
- **REPLICATED:** independent reproduction of a specified test with comparable outcomes.
- **INFERRED:** reasoned interpretation, clearly separated from observed facts.
- **PROPOSED:** question or protocol for future testing; no measurements.
- **UNKNOWN:** inadequate evidence to classify.

Each substantive record must display its tier, origin, source, date, limitation and current state. Do not quietly upgrade an evidence tier.

## Falsifiability

A null or negative result is a valid result. Human-only baselines and model-only baselines matter. Do not retroactively edit a task's definition to match a result. Report failures, exclusions, costs and caveats.

## Publication boundaries

Publication does not establish model sentience, machine personhood, medical efficacy, broad population effects, or intellectual-property ownership. Do not claim such findings without appropriate methods and evidence.

## Safety & privacy

Use only authorized code/data. Human experiments require consent and appropriate review for the study context. The initial site collects no user data and performs no AI inference.
